Why I built this

I've spent most of my career in cross-border payments, and for the last few years the loudest claim in the industry has been that stablecoins made moving money cheap. I couldn't find anyone who had actually priced that claim off live order books — not a quote, not a blog benchmark, the actual execution cost, decomposed. So I built a collector that does.

Along the way it turned into two instruments: the corridor I originally wanted the receipt for, and a wider board that fell out of the method — if you're already comparing USDT prices to official dollar rates in one country, you can do it in ten.

What it measures

The basis board. Every hour, the price of USDT in local currency on each country's main exchange, against the official USD rate at the same moment. The gap, in basis points. Positive — people pay a premium over the official rate for a dollar; negative — USDT trades below it.

How it's measured — and what isn't

Every number is one of three things:

measured Live order books walked at real sizes, official mids, incumbent quotes — timestamped, kept raw.
assumed {{ assumedNote }}{{ feeStamp }}
not visible Enterprise pricing (Nium, Thunes, Circle partners), OTC execution — stated, never estimated.

One correction is part of the record: my first version had the patient-execution route beating the incumbent 3×. Verifying the venues' published fee schedules killed it — there is no zero-fee maker tier. The correction stays documented, because the site only works if the numbers survive someone checking.

Built to fail loudly
Failed pulls are written as failures, gaps show as gaps, and the scheduler goes red when a sample is incomplete — an earlier version of this project died silently for 34 days, and that's the failure mode everything here is designed against.
