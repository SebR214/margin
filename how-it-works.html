How it works

This site measures two things. The real price of a dollar in Loading… countries, and what it really costs to send money using stablecoins (digital dollars) instead of an app like Wise. Every number below is computed from a file you can open, not typed in by hand.
How we price a country
Every hour, we go out and get real offers, what people are actually asking for a dollar right now, in that country's own currency. We take the typical price you would actually pay, compare it to the official exchange rate captured in the same hour, and the gap between the two is the number this site publishes. Nothing is modelled or smoothed. If the real offers move, the number moves.
The rule is ten real sell offers, at minimum, before we'll publish a price. Under that, the country shows its reason instead of a number. Loading today's counts…
How we price sending money

The app prices on the same page come from two places. Where an app quotes its own price directly, we use that. Where it doesn't, we fall back to a public comparison feed that Wise itself publishes, which is a real quote but not the app's own, and we say which one it is on every row.
What was hard
Fees hide inside the exchange rate, and they change with how much you send, so one headline fee number is almost always wrong. Every cost on this site is all-in, measured at five real amounts, which is the only way the comparison means anything.
History cannot be gotten back. An hour that never gets collected is gone, you cannot go back later and fill it in. So keeping the hourly check running has mattered more than shipping any single feature.
A pipeline like this will happily produce a plausible number when the real data is missing, and that would defeat the entire point of this site. So nothing gets published unless it traces to a file that was actually collected, and the attempts that failed stay visible instead of being smoothed away.
