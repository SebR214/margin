We measure two things every hour: what a dollar really costs, and what sending money costs

What a dollar costs: the price of USDT, divided by the official rate

Every hour we take the middle price of the 10 cheapest ads selling USDT in each country, on Binance P2P or a local exchange, and divide it by the official rate. The number you see is the median of the last 24 hours. A country with fewer than 10 sellers gets no price, and we say why.

What sending money costs: what arrives, compared with the real exchange rate

For each provider we compare what the recipient gets with what they would get at the mid-market rate, at five amounts. Airwallex, Instarem, Wise and WorldRemit quote us directly. The rest come from a public price comparison. The stablecoin route is priced on real exchange order books, with every fee included.

What we don't measure yet

  • The official rate only updates once a day, so it can be up to 24 hours behind the market.
  • Bank fees for getting money onto and off the exchanges.
  • Prices for repeat senders. Some app prices are first-transfer offers.
  • Cash dollars at money changers.

How what people pay for a dollar is made
Every hour we collect live offers to sell dollars, take the median, and compare it to the official rate captured in the same check. Nothing is modelled or smoothed. A country needs at least 10 sell ads, or a real exchange order book, to get a price.

What was hard
Fees. They hide inside the exchange rate and they change with how much you send, so one headline fee number is almost always wrong. Everything here is all-in, measured at five different amounts, which is the only way the comparison means anything.
History. An hour that does not get collected is gone, you cannot go back and fill it in. So keeping collection running has mattered more than shipping any feature.
Made-up numbers. An automated pipeline will happily produce a plausible figure when the data is missing, and that would kill the whole point of this. So nothing gets published unless it traces to a file that was actually collected, and the attempts that failed stay up.
The code and every data file are public. Your AI can ask the same questions over MCP or the API.
