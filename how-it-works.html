How it works

Most currency sites show the official exchange rate. In a lot of countries that is not what people actually pay. The street price of a dollar can be a few percent off the official rate, or a thousand percent off, and the official number hides it completely. This site measures that difference, every hour, in 59 countries. It also prices what it really costs to send money on four corridors, fees and exchange-rate margin together.
How a number gets made
Every hour the collector goes out and gets real offers, what people are asking for a dollar right now. It takes the median price you would actually pay, compares it to the official rate captured in the same pass, and the gap is the number. Nothing is modelled or smoothed. If the offers move, the number moves.
The rule is ten sell offers minimum. Under that, the country shows its reason instead of a price. Loading today's counts…
Where the prices come from
All of it is collected directly, every hour, and written to files you can open:
· Binance P2P, live buy and sell ads, the main source for most countries
· Order books on twelve exchanges: Upbit, Bithumb and Coinone in Korea, Indodax and Pintu in Indonesia, BTCTurk and Paribu in Turkey, Bitkub in Thailand, Coins.ph in the Philippines, Independent Reserve in Australia, Foxbit and Mercado Bitcoin in Brazil
· CoinGecko, a backup price when a venue is down
· open.er-api, the official exchange rate, captured in the same second as the market price
· BCRA, Argentina's central bank, for its published parallel rate
· Airwallex and Instarem, live quotes pulled from their own pricing at five different transfer amounts
· Wise's public comparison, for the other transfer providers including Wise itself
Every number on the site links back to the file it came from. Nothing is typed in by hand.
What was hard
Fees. They hide inside the exchange rate and they change with how much you send, so one headline fee number is almost always wrong. Everything here is all-in, measured at five different amounts, which is the only way the comparison means anything.
History. An hour that does not get collected is gone, you cannot go back and fill it in. So keeping collection running has mattered more than shipping any feature.
Made-up numbers. An automated pipeline will happily produce a plausible figure when the data is missing, and that would kill the whole point of this. So nothing gets published unless it traces to a file that was actually collected, and the attempts that failed stay up.
The code and every data file are public. Your AI can ask the same questions over MCP or the API.
