No number is typed in by hand. When we miss an hour, the chart shows a gap. Every point you see was really collected.

A dollar's price is the middle of real offers, every hour

Each country gets one price for a dollar every hour, built like this.

  • A stablecoin is a coin that stays worth one US dollar.
  • We take the 10 cheapest offers to sell one on the exchange people there use. The middle offer is the price, so one odd seller cannot move it.
  • We set that price against the official rate at the same moment. The percentage is how much more a dollar really costs than that rate says.
  • A positive percentage means people pay more than the official rate. A negative one means they pay less.
  • With fewer than 10 sellers we publish no price. You see a gap, never a guess.

Sending cost is the money lost against the mid-market rate

We price each app and the stablecoin route at five amounts, with the exchange fees and the fee for moving the coin counted. Look at the one closest to what you send.

The amounts are 200, 1,000, 5,000, 25,000 and 50,000, in the currency you send from.

  • The mid-market rate is the middle price banks trade at with each other.
  • Whatever your recipient gets below that rate is what sending costs you.

Some costs are not in our figures, so check these yourself

  • The official rate updates only once a day. On days a currency moves fast, the gap we show can be a day behind.
  • Some exchanges do not publish what your bank charges to send money there, so we leave it out. Check your own bank's fee before you compare.
  • Some app prices are offers for a first transfer. If you have used that app before, you may pay more than we show.
  • We measure stablecoins only. If you are buying cash at a money changer, treat our numbers as a guide, not a quote.
