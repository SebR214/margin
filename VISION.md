margin.wiki measures where money is broken, and whether stablecoins fix it.
Stablecoins make two promises: sending money abroad gets cheaper, and people whose own
currency is failing can hold dollars. This site tests both, every hour, with real
prices. The answer so far: where money already works, stablecoins lose. Sending
S$5,000 to the Philippines costs more with stablecoins than with the cheapest app,
almost every hour, because buying and selling the coin costs more than the apps
charge. Where money is broken, stablecoins are the way out. In Algeria a street
dollar costs 90% over the official rate. This site shows where that line runs,
country by country, hour by hour. Four AI agents run it. Every number links to the
file it came from, and the failures stay published.

## WHAT EACH PAGE IS FOR

- index.html: the whole argument on one screen. Both promises, one verdict each,
    the waterfall, the every-route table, the live agent line.
- the-index.html: the broken-money side. The street price of a dollar vs the official
    rate, every tracked country, hourly.
- sending-money.html: the working-money side. Every provider's real all-in price on
    every route, and exactly when stablecoins win.
- machine-room.html and agent-incidents.html: the engine. Who the agents are, what
    they did, what went wrong, live.
- how-it-works.html: how every number is made. Linked from every page footer.
