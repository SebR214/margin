# How to read this site

Prices from the 04:00 UTC hour, 25 Oct.

## What we measure

Every country has an official exchange rate. In some countries banks won’t sell dollars at that rate, or sell very few, so people buy them from each other and pay more. Every hour we check what people really pay for a dollar in 54 countries and compare it with the official rate.

## What the percentage means

In Algeria, the official rate says a dollar costs 135 DZD. People actually pay 260 DZD. That’s 93.1% more, so Algeria shows +93.1%.

0% means people pay the official rate, and most countries are close to it. A minus means they pay a little less.

## Where the price comes from

We get it from local exchanges first, then brokers, then people selling to each other on Binance and OKX. What they sell is mostly stablecoins, digital coins that are always worth one US dollar, so their price is the price of a dollar. If there aren’t enough prices in an hour, that country shows no price. We never guess.

## The cost of sending money

On 11 routes we also check what it costs to send money abroad.

We compare transfer apps with sending it by stablecoin. The cost is how much less the person receiving gets than at the official rate, with every fee we can see included. This hour, sending S$5,000 to the Philippines costs S$23.70 with Wise, the cheapest app. Where a fee can't be seen, the route says so.

<https://margin.wiki/data/routes_hourly.json>

## What our numbers leave out

- A day behind. The official rate updates only once a day. On days a currency moves fast, the gap we show can be a day behind.
- Your bank's fee. Some exchanges do not publish what your bank charges to send money there, so we leave it out. Check your own bank's fee before you compare.
- First-transfer offers. Some app prices are offers for a first transfer. If you have used that app before, you may pay more than we show.
- Cash is different. We measure stablecoins only. If you are buying cash at a money changer, treat our numbers as a guide, not a quote.
