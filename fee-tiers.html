Stablecoins are cheap, kind of

Moving the coin costs almost nothing, but getting in and out is where most of the money goes, and which route actually wins mostly comes down to a fee schedule most people sending money never read.
The thing everyone says

The pitch for stablecoins in cross border is that they settle in seconds, cost next to nothing and skip the chain of correspondent banks that makes a wire slow and expensive, and if you only look at moving the coin from one wallet to another that's true — I measured it live on Singapore to the Philippines sending S$5,000 and the move itself costs 0.10% of the transfer. That's the number people tend to quote, but it's not really the one that tells you whether sending money this way is actually cheap.

Moving the coin is a fraction of a percent, and buying and selling it is the rest, and all of that is either a published fee or a real gap in the market price at that moment — nothing in there is estimated.

Where I got it wrong first

The fair pushback is that a market order means you pay the worst price on both sides, so what happens if you set your own price and wait for it to fill instead? An earlier version of this page said that changes everything and that a limit order beat the best app by about 3×, which was wrong, and I think the way it was wrong is more useful than the corrected number.

The earlier draft treated the limit order as free, on the logic that a posted order doesn't cross the spread, which is right, but you still pay a fee: Independent Reserve charges the same flat 0.50% whether you use a limit order or a market order, and Coins.ph charges 0.10% for a limit order vs 0.15% for a market order. Once both sides are priced on the real fee schedules the difference is pretty small — the same S$5,000 costs about 0.80% with a limit order, so you save around 0.05 percentage points, nowhere near 3×.

At published fees the stablecoin route loses to the best app either way, at every size the site measures. I'm leaving the correction up because the whole point of the site is that numbers get checked rather than assumed, which has to include checking my own headline number. In the end the fix was just reading the fee schedules properly.
The one corridor where it's close

Of the four corridors I price at this level of detail (the original 4 corridors, as of Sep), three lose to the best app in pretty much every hour I've measured — Singapore, Australia and New Zealand to the Philippines all sit at or close to a 0% win rate with a market order. US to Mexico is different, and a limit order there wins 42.9% of measured hours at $5,000.

The reason is mostly Bitso on the Mexican side — their fees are quite a bit tighter than Coins.ph's, and they publish their volume tiers as actual data I can pull directly rather than something scraped off a page, so it's a real difference in what the two exchanges charge and not just a lucky hour.

So it's really a volume question

I still think stablecoins can be a good way to move money, but "stablecoins are cheap" is usually said about the rail, and that's not really the question you have when you're sending money — which is whether it beats what you'd pay otherwise — and that mostly comes down to the volume discounts every exchange publishes and almost nobody reads.

For Singapore to the Philippines I measured where it flips, and the stablecoin route only starts winning at payments-company volume: around — a month with a market order, or — a month with a limit order, which is the volume where Independent Reserve drops from the 0.50% base rate everyone starts on to its — tiers. Below that it loses, and for a regular sender it isn't the cheaper option yet — at least on this corridor.
